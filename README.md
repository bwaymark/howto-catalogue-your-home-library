# How to catalogue your home library

This is how I catalogued about 800 books at home using the Dewey Decimal Classification. It covers both the shelves and an old-fashioned card catalogue. The aim is that every book has a Dewey number on its spine, the shelves are in number order, and any book can be found either in LibraryThing or in a drawer of cards filed by author, title and subject.

It's written for a home library of a few hundred to a few thousand books. Public libraries have far more books than that, so some of what they do doesn't make sense here, and the guide says so where it matters.

## 2. Catalogue the books in LibraryThing

1. Add every book to LibraryThing. Scanning ISBN barcodes is quickest. For books without an ISBN, search by title and author.
2. Check the **Dewey Decimal** field. LibraryThing fills it from library records for most books (530 of my 535 got a number this way). It may be missing for self-published, very old or obscure books.
3. Tidy author names as you go. LibraryThing sometimes imports names in odd forms, such as `PhD, Nicole Forsgren`, `strauss-neil` or `Dweck, Dr Carol`. Fix them in LibraryThing to *Surname, Forename*, because both the spine codes and the author cards depend on them.
4. Subject headings are optional. LibraryThing's subject field was only filled for about a quarter of my books, so the card catalogue uses Dewey captions for subjects instead (see section 6).

## 3. Assign Dewey numbers

Dewey splits knowledge into ten main classes:

| Class | Subject |
|---|---|
| 000 | Computer science, information & general works |
| 100 | Philosophy & psychology |
| 200 | Religion |
| 300 | Social sciences |
| 400 | Language |
| 500 | Science |
| 600 | Technology (incl. cookery, DIY, gardening, management) |
| 700 | Arts & recreation (incl. sport and walking) |
| 800 | Literature |
| 900 | History & geography (incl. travel and biography) |

Each class is split into ten divisions and each division into ten sections, so 942 is *History of England & Wales*. Digits after the decimal point narrow the subject further: 942.52 is Nottinghamshire.

### Rules I followed

- **Use LibraryThing's number unless it's clearly wrong.** It comes from real library records. Where I'd have picked something different (LibraryThing has Bryson's *At Home* at 643.1, housing, where 392.36, domestic customs, is also defensible), I kept LibraryThing's number so the shelves and the records agree.
- **Cut numbers to three decimal places at most.** 658.4092 becomes 658.409. With fewer than a few thousand books, longer numbers add nothing to the shelf order and are hard to read on a spine.
- **Decide on geographic suffixes once.** Walking guides can all go at 796.51, or be split by area (796.51094235 for Devon). Pubs can be 647.95 or 647.9542 for England. Either works, but apply the choice consistently.
- **If a book spans two subjects, shelve it where you'd look for it first.** *Strange Nottingham* (ghosts, UFOs, oddities) went to 133.1, ghosts, because that's its main topic.
- **Group series together.** The *Twelve Quakers and…* series all go at 289.6 (Quakers), even though one or two titles could be classed as theology at 230.96.

### Fiction

Dewey classes novels by the language they were written in: 823 English, 813 American, 843 French, 833 German and so on. Most UK public libraries ignore this. They shelve fiction separately, alphabetically by author, with labels like `F BRY`. Either approach works at home:

- **All Dewey.** Novels sit at 823 and similar with everything else. This is consistent, but 823 becomes one very long run.
- **Separate fiction sequence.** Novels are labelled `F` plus an author code and shelved A–Z. This is easier to browse.

Pick one before printing any spine labels.

## 4. Label the spines

Each spine label has two lines: the Dewey number, with the first three letters of the author's surname underneath.

```
942.52
ARM
```

The author code puts books with the same number in order. Without it, the four books at 942 are in no particular order. When there's no author, as with some guidebooks and anthologies, use the first three letters of the title, ignoring *The*, *A* and *An*.

Put labels at the same height on every spine, about 2 cm from the bottom, so the shelves look tidy and the numbers line up.

## 5. Shelve in Dewey order

Shelve left to right, top to bottom, in Dewey order, treating the number as a decimal rather than a whole number:

```
641.5  →  641.52  →  641.595  →  641.6
```

Within one number, sort by author code, then by title.

## 6. Build the card catalogue

A traditional card catalogue has several cards for each book, filed in separate sequences:

| Sequence | Filed by | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| **Author** | Surname | Find everything by one author |
| **Added author** | Co-author or editor surname | Find books where the person isn't the main author |
| **Title** | Title, ignoring *The/A/An* | Find a book when you only know the title |
| **Subject** | Dewey number | Browse everything on a subject |

Libraries also kept a **shelf list** in shelf order, but with only a few shelves the shelves themselves do that job.

Every card carries the call number (Dewey plus author code), so wherever you start, the card tells you where the book is.

My 535 books needed 1,657 cards: 523 author, 126 added author, 535 title and 473 subject. The rough rule is about three cards per book.

### Subject cards from Dewey

Instead of free-text subject headings, each subject card is headed with the three-digit Dewey section and its caption, for example `942 History of England & Wales` or `641 Food & drink`. Subject cards are filed in number order. This is a *classified catalogue*, the arrangement Dewey was designed for. It works even when LibraryThing has no subject data.

Fiction (813, 823, 833, 843) gets no subject cards, because "English fiction" would just repeat the shelf order.

### Making the merge file

Don't write 1,600 cards by hand. Export your library from LibraryThing (**More › Import/Export › Export to Excel**) and run the script in this repository:

```bash
pip install pandas openpyxl
python scripts/make_catalogue_cards.py LibraryThing_export.xlsx Catalogue_cards_merge.xlsx
```

It produces a workbook with:

- **Cards**: one row per card, in filing order. Columns: catalogue, heading, call number, author code, author, title, imprint, pages, Dewey subject, Dewey main class and LibraryThing ID.
- **Summary**: card counts per catalogue and the number of DK rolls needed.
- **No Dewey**: books that still need a number.

The script cuts Dewey numbers to three decimal places, builds author codes, files titles without leading articles (using LibraryThing's *Sort Character* field), strips titles such as Dr and Professor from author names, and adds Dewey section captions. Any names it can't fix can be added to the `NAMEFIX` dictionary at the top of the script, but it's better to correct them in LibraryThing.

### Printing on the QL-710W

1. In **P-touch Editor**, create a layout of 62 mm × 115 mm on DK-22205 continuous tape.
2. Connect the merge file with **File › Database › Connect** and choose the `Cards` sheet.
3. Drag the fields onto the layout. Mine looks like this:

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ 942.52                                      │
│ ARM      Armitage, Jill                     │  ← heading (bold)
│                                             │
│          Nottingham: A History              │
│          Amberley Publishing (2015) · 128 pp│
│                                             │
│          942 History of England & Wales     │  ← Dewey subject
└─────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

4. Print one catalogue at a time. The rows come out in filing order, so the cards drop straight into the drawer.
5. Stick each label on the card with about 7 mm of margin top and bottom.

One 30.48 m roll of DK-22205 makes about 265 labels at 115 mm, so 1,650 cards need seven rolls.

**Alternative:** if your ordinary printer handles card stock, a Word mail merge onto 5 × 3 card sheets is cheaper and avoids sticking labels by hand. Labels are better for adding a few cards at a time as new books arrive.

**Durability:** DK labels are direct thermal and fade in sunlight. In a closed drawer that doesn't matter.

### Cards

Library catalogue cards are 5 × 3 in (127 × 76 mm). Buy plain white record cards in that size. 6 × 4 cards won't fit a library drawer. Recipe cards work if they're 5 × 3, but they're often thicker (about 300 gsm). With a label added, that's about 0.45 mm per card against about 0.3 mm for a plain card with a label, so you get fewer cards per drawer.

## 7. Choose a cabinet

### Sizing

Work out the length of cards, not the number:

| Card | Thickness with label | 3,000 cards |
|---|---|---|
| Plain 5 × 3 record card | ~0.3–0.35 mm | ~0.9–1 m |
| Heavy recipe card | ~0.45 mm | ~1.3 m |

Drawers should be no more than about three-quarters full, so you can still flick through. A drawer about 40 cm deep holds 700–800 labelled record cards. For 3,000 cards, aim for six drawers. That also lets you give the author, title and subject sequences two drawers each.

Inside, each drawer needs to be at least 130 mm wide and 80 mm tall.

### What I bought

A second-hand **Libraco** six-drawer oak catalogue (two across, three high), roughly 33 cm wide, 40 cm high and 48 cm deep with a sloping front. It was listed at £99.50, collection only. Comparable cabinets on eBay were listed at £220–£600 plus delivery.

Its drawers have no card rods. Rods ran through a hole in the bottom of each card to stop cards being pulled out or spilt. Without them, cards can be lifted out freely, and you don't need to punch the cards. A follower block (a sliding wooden block) or a stiff divider at the back keeps a part-full drawer upright.

### Buying tips

- **Search terms.** On eBay, Facebook Marketplace, Gumtree and [the-saleroom.com](https://www.the-saleroom.com), search for "library card index", "card catalogue drawers" and "Libraco".
- **Avoid "apothecary card catalog" listings.** The new £30 "16-drawer apothecary card catalog holder" units are decorative trinket boxes, and their drawers are far too small for 5 × 3 cards.
- **Ask for measurements.** Get the *internal* width, height and depth of a drawer before you buy. Some continental cabinets take 7.5 × 12.5 cm or A7 cards instead.
- **Check the fittings and condition.** Look for drawer stops, rods if you want them, follower blocks, woodworm and sticking drawers.
- **Plan collection.** Oak cabinets are heavy and often collection only.

## 8. Adding new books

1. Add the book to LibraryThing and check the Dewey number and author name.
2. Print the spine label on the P-touch and shelve the book.
3. Print its cards. Either re-export and filter the merge file by LibraryThing ID, or type the details into a single-card P-touch layout.
4. File each card in its sequence.

## 9. Lessons learned

- **Decide the rules before printing anything:** decimal places, geographic suffixes and how fiction is handled. Reprinting spine labels is tedious.
- **LibraryThing's data is good but not clean.** Fix author names there, not in the merge file, or they'll come back on the next export.
- **Dewey subject captions are better than free-text subject headings** for a home catalogue. They're always present, consistent, and file in a logical order.
- **Measure everything:** card size, drawer interiors and the length of the card run.
